# Parents of Young Children: Why Your Screen Time Matters, Too

Parents today are more pressed for time than any other generation. Smartphones can connect us and make life easier, but **too much tech and too little talk can delay communication skills development**.

The most important thing for your child’s development is **you**. Separating from devices and focusing on your baby, toddler, or preschooler is essential.

---

## Why YOU Can't Be Replaced by Technology

- **80% of brain development occurs by age 3**.  
- Speech and language skills are linked to thinking, social relationships, reading, writing, and school success.  
- Development happens through **real-life interactions**—both verbal and nonverbal.  

Nothing replaces these interactions. Focus on **quality face-to-face time**.

---

## 3 Screen-Free Ways to Engage with Young Children

### 1. Play Together
- Games like peekaboo, pat-a-cake, and *Itsy-Bitsy Spider* promote face-to-face interaction.  
- Teach turn-taking, bonding, and conversation skills.  
- Activities like blowing kisses, waving bye-bye, and clapping build social interaction.  

### 2. Share a Common Focus
- Read a book, share a toy, or look at the same object together.  
- Builds **joint attention**, a vital communication skill.  
- Shows children you are interested in what they say or do.  

### 3. Serve and Return
- Communication includes **non-verbal signals**: eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, body language.  
- Parents distracted by phones may miss these cues.  
- Respond to signals—like tossing back a ball—to show children their actions matter.  

---

## Tips for Managing Tech Overload

- **Create regular tech-free times**: Especially before bedtime.  
- **Limit distractions**: Make tech-free zones like the kitchen table.  
- **Designate tech-free outings**: Park trips, zoo visits, or pool days without phones.  
- **Use technology interactively**: Engage with kids face-to-face while using devices.  
- **Set timers**: Avoid endless scrolling by setting reminders to reengage.  

---

## Remember: Your Young Child Looks to You

Communication skills are critical to development.  
Children learn through **listening, talking, reading, singing, and playing** with parents.  

Smartphones are useful, but **find time to interact without them**.  
Your child may not thank you now, but you’ll be thankful you helped them grow and learn.
