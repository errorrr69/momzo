# 8 Parenting Goals to Start the New Year Strong

New Year's resolutions are often sweeping and grand. But you can often reap the biggest rewards by building off strengths already in place. Helping to make your family safer, stronger, and more harmonious in the coming year may not require a complete overhaul, but rather a few strategic tweaks.

The start of a new year is a great time to take a step back, take a breath, and look at how you are taking care of yourself and others in your family.  
- **Celebrate what you’re already doing right!**  
- Then, consider these suggestions for a healthy new year.  

*(Kids can have fun setting goals, too! See Healthy New Year's Resolutions for Children and Teens.)*

---

## 1. Get Everyone Up-to-Date on Recommended Immunizations
- Vaccines protect against dangerous viruses such as RSV, flu, and COVID.  
- Call your pediatrician to ensure your children are up-to-date.  
- Ask questions and remind kids about good hand hygiene habits to prevent the spread of germs.  

---

## 2. Build Healthy Digital Habits
- Research age-appropriate media.  
- Make or update a **Family Media Plan**.  
- Prevent gaming from becoming unhealthy.  
- Screen time shouldn’t always be alone time—watch shows or play games together.  

---

## 3. Read Together
- Set aside daily time for shared reading.  
- For younger kids: build reading into bedtime routines.  
- For older kids/teens: share a favorite book or listen to audiobooks together.  
- Reading boosts brain development and strengthens parent-child bonds.  

---

## 4. Get Outside More
- Outdoor time boosts mood and provides physical activity and vitamin D.  
- Nature time gives kids’ eyes a healthy break from screens.  
- Helps improve sleep quality.  

---

## 5. Check Car Seat Limits for Safety
- Kids grow quickly and may outgrow car seats faster than expected.  
- Keep children rear-facing as long as possible, up to the seat’s limits.  
- This commonly includes children under 2 and most up to age 4.  
- Check for new car seat laws in your state.  
- Remind caregivers of all safety rules.  

---

## 6. Set Aside Time to Cook Together & Share Family Meals
- Continue holiday traditions of cooking together into the new year.  
- Cooking together can encourage picky eaters to try new foods.  
- Eating meals together at least 3 times a week provides physical, emotional, social, and academic benefits.  

---

## 7. Make a Family Disaster Kit
- Extreme weather events are becoming more frequent due to climate change.  
- Being prepared reduces fear.  
- Ask children what they’d want in a disaster kit.  
- Assemble basic supplies together.  
- Include useful items like food, water, flashlights, and first aid essentials.
