# 10 No-Cost, Screen-Free Activities to Play with Your Preschooler

---

## Getting Back to Basics

Most parents want to provide more for their children than their parents were able to do for them. But have you ever noticed how kids tend to have fun with things as simple as a cardboard box? It's true.  

The **American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP)** report on the importance of play explains that inexpensive toys such as blocks, balls, jump ropes, and buckets are more effective in allowing children to be imaginative and creative than more expensive toys that may be out of reach for many parents.  

So, why not take that cardboard box and play with them? More than any material gift, **YOU are the best toy your child will ever receive!**

---

## Not Just Kids' Stuff

While it may be hard to relax and give yourself over to play, view this time with your child as an adventure. You are not only promoting the many benefits of play, but also getting to know your child better and strengthening the parent-child bond.  

For starters, your role can actually be quite minimal and the play you undertake can be almost any activity.

Here are ten ideas that your preschooler will adore.  
**Bonus:** You don't need much time or expensive lessons or toys to participate in any of the activities listed!

---

## 10 Screen-Free Activities

### 1. Duck, Duck, Goose
- Everyone sits in a circle.  
- One child is "It" and taps others saying "Duck."  
- At their discretion, they tap someone and say "Goose."  
- The "Goose" must chase "It" around the circle.  
- If "It" sits down safely, they remain safe. If tagged, they’re out.  
- The game continues until one child remains.  

---

### 2. London Bridge is Falling Down
- Two children form a bridge by joining hands.  
- Others pass under while singing the rhyme.  
- When the song ends, the arms drop around the child passing through.  
- The song changes to *“Take the key and lock him up.”*  
- Preschoolers delight in being “locked up” and swayed to and fro.  

---

### 3. Limbo
- Use a broomstick held by two older children or adults.  
- Kids go under the stick without touching it.  
- Lower the stick gradually after each round.  
- Add music for extra fun.  

---

### 4. Egg Races
- Hard boil eggs and give kids tablespoons.  
- Challenge them to walk, run, or jump while balancing the egg.  
- Promotes balance and dexterity.  

---

### 5. Simon Says
- Encourages listening skills and focus.  
- You are Simon—give commands like *“Simon says touch your nose.”*  
- If you don’t say “Simon says,” anyone who follows is out.  
- The last child standing wins.  

---

### 6. Head, Shoulders, Knees, and Toes
- Sing the tune and control the pace.  
- Children touch the body part as it’s mentioned.  
- Speed up the pace for extra fun and laughter.  

---

### 7. Nature Walks
- Turn any walk into a nature adventure.  
- Observe weather, animals, bugs, and plants.  
- Example: *“Look at those big clouds,”* or *“Touch this grass—it’s wet from yesterday’s rain.”*  
- Preschoolers love exploring and asking questions.  

---

### 8. Follow the Leader
- Move around doing different actions.  
- Everyone copies your movements.  
- Simple, great fun!  

---

### 9. Tag
- You start as "It."  
- Others try to catch and tag you.  
- Whoever tags you becomes "It."  
- Designate safe spots like trees or benches.  

---

### 10. Run Around
- Call out fun challenges:  
  - *“Run from this tree to that tree.”*  
  - *“Hop on one foot from this bench to that tree.”*  
- Endless suggestions—your preschooler won’t get bored!  

---

## Remember…

While you may find many opportunities to capitalize on “teachable moments” during these activities, the key is to do what comes naturally to you as a parent.  

Playing together shouldn’t be a chore or something you feel pressured to do. **Enjoy the time you spend with your child—it will pass all too soon!**
