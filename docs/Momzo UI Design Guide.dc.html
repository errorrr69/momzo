UI Design Guide
A clean, cozy, happy system for busy moms.
The visual language behind Momzo — colors, type, components and tone that make learning about your child feel calm, warm, and never one more thing to fail at.
Version 1.0 · June 2026 · Mobile (iOS / Android)
01 · Brand Personality
How Momzo should feel
Every screen is for a tired, time-poor, slightly guilty mom. The design's whole job is to make her feel capable and close to her child — calm, warm, and instantly clear. Nothing should add pressure.
Cozy, not clinical
Warm cream backgrounds, soft shadows, rounded corners. It should feel like a kitchen table, not a dashboard.
Happy, not hyper
Cheerful colour used in moments of delight — never a wall of saturation. Joy in small doses.
Calm, not cluttered
One clear job per screen. Generous space. A mom should never feel lost or overwhelmed.
Kind, never shaming
Celebrate small wins, never punish a miss. No streaks-as-pressure, no red alarms, no guilt.
Design principles
01
Five minutes, one decision. Each screen has a single obvious next action.
02
Personal, always. Use the child's name and real behaviours; never feel generic.
03
Big, friendly touch targets. One-handed, on the go, minimum 44px.
04
Warm words over jargon. Speak like a kind friend who happens to be an expert.

03 · Typography
Two voices, working together
A warm serif for emotional, human moments; a clean rounded sans for everything functional. The mix is what makes Momzo feel personal rather than corporate.
Newsreader
Emotional headers · quotes · "feel" moments
Used italic for warmth. Big titles, child's name, reflective copy. Regular & medium weights only.
Nunito Sans
UI · buttons · labels · body
The workhorse. Rounded, friendly, highly legible at small sizes. Weights 400–900.
Type scale
Display / heroNewsreader · 26–32px · screen heroes
Screen titleNunito 900 · 24–28px
Card headingNunito 800 · 17–21px
Body — the everyday reading size for moms.Nunito 400/600 · 15–16px · 1.5 line
Eyebrow / labelNunito 800 · 11–12px · uppercase
Meta / timestampNunito 700 · 12px · muted
Rule: body text never drops below 15px. Moms read this on the move, often tired — legibility beats density every time.

06 · Voice & Tone
Like a kind friend who happens to be an expert
The words carry as much of the brand as the colours. Warm, plain, encouraging — and absolutely never a source of guilt.
We say
"You connected 3 days this week — lovely. 🌱"
"It's okay if he stays upset. Staying calm yourself is the lesson."
"Something happening right now? Let's find a calm way through."
We never say
"You broke your streak! Don't lose your progress."
"You haven't opened Momzo in 3 days."
"Your child is falling behind."
Tone rules
Celebrate, never shame. Every nudge frames a small win, not a failure.
Short & concrete. A tired mom gets a script, not an essay.
Always personal. Use the child's name; reference real moments.
Safe by default. On hard topics, lead with warmth and refer to a professional — never diagnose.
"A parenting app that makes a tired mom feel worse will be deleted. Everything here is built to make her feel a little more capable, and a lot more close."
