# 14 Ways to Show Your Child Love: Valentine's Day & Every Day

Feeling loved is key to a child's healthy mental and emotional development. Showing your child love, in a variety of ways, will help model what a loving relationship is to them and set a foundation for them to build loving relationships with others.

There are many ways to show you care that go beyond candy and cards. Here are 14 tips for Valentine’s Day—or any day.

---

## 1. Cue the Cuddles
From the moment they're born, little ones love being held, cuddled, and caressed.  
- Gentle touches during feeding, diapering, and rocking help them feel secure.  
- Consider adding baby massage to your routine.  
- Research shows physical touch is essential to health and emotional development.  

---

## 2. Share the Love of Reading
- Start reading to your child in infancy.  
- Reading strengthens parent-child bonds and promotes positive parenting.  
- Builds a foundation for school success and long-term wellness.  

---

## 3. Think Hugs First
- When your child is angry or upset, calm yourself first.  
- Offer a hug, cuddle, pat, or nod of affection.  
- Once calm, talk about the event and how to manage emotions next time.  
- Use **emotion coaching** to help kids understand and regulate feelings.  

---

## 4. Discipline With Love
- Use positive, non-violent discipline.  
- Harsh punishments can damage long-term health.  
- Provide clear and consistent rules.  
- Praise good behavior, calmly explain consequences, and follow through.  

---

## 5. Hold Time Together Dear
- Time spent together shows love.  
- Schedule family activities like game nights or outdoor fun.  
- Carve out one-on-one time with each child.  
- Be present—put away phones and devices.  
- Co-viewing media (like family movie night) can spark conversations.  

---

## 6. Embrace Health & Safety
- Take children to regular well-child visits.  
- Keep vaccines up-to-date.  
- Provide nutritious food, sleep, and exercise.  
- Create a safe home environment.  
- Always use seat belts or car seats.  

---

## 7. Choose Words with Care
- Use positive and encouraging words.  
- Model gratitude with “please” and “thank you.”  
- Avoid sarcasm, mockery, or put-downs.  
- Negative language can harm self-esteem and relationships.  

---

## 8. Forgive Mistakes, Including Your Own
- Show love and grace to yourself and your child.  
- If you lose your cool, apologize and explain how you’ll handle it differently.  
- Forgive yourself—no one is perfect.  
- Teach children to accept mistakes and build resilience.  

---

## 9. Care for the Earth
- Spend time together in nature.  
- Show children how caring for the environment protects their future.  
- Discuss climate-change concerns honestly and with hope.  

---

## 10. Cook Up Cherished Moments
- Cook together to teach healthy food choices.  
- Involve kids in planning, shopping, preparing, and serving meals.  
- Family meals are opportunities to connect—put away devices.  

---

## 11. Foster Friendships
- Help children build positive relationships with friends, siblings, and community.  
- Teach kindness and teamwork.  
- Encourage involvement in group activities like sports.  
- Get to know your child’s friends and discuss respectful relationships.  

---

## 12. Have Heart-to-Heart Conversations
- Ask, *“How was your day?”* and listen actively.  
- Be available when your child wants to talk.  
- Let them finish sharing before offering solutions.  
- Watch for signs of anxiety or depression and consult a pediatrician if needed.  

---

## 13. Let Your Child Know You Love Them No Matter Who They Love
- Tell teens they can talk to you about crushes.  
- Discuss dating, relationships, gender identity, and sexual activity.  
- Teach respect for themselves and others—*“no means no.”*  

---

## 14. Share These 3 Words Without Limit
- All children want their parent’s attention, no matter their age.  
- Make time daily to talk and connect.  
- Staying connected helps kids make healthy choices.  
- Say *“I love you”* often—kids are never too old to hear it.  

---
