# Helping your child feel they matter

**"Mattering" — a simple idea with huge impact.** Mattering is a child's deep sense that they are
significant — that they are noticed, valued, and important to the people around them, and that they
*add* something to others' lives. It sounds soft, but it's one of the most powerful protective factors
in a child's development. Children who feel they matter have stronger self-worth, better mental health,
more resilience, and a greater sense of purpose and belonging. Children who feel they *don't* matter —
invisible, unimportant, replaceable — are far more vulnerable to anxiety, depression, loneliness, and
acting out. Much misbehaviour, in fact, is a child trying to matter in the only way that's working.

**Mattering has two sides.** A child needs to feel both:
1. **"I am valued"** — noticed, cared about, listened to, important to my people just for being me.
2. **"I add value"** — I contribute, I'm needed, I make a difference to others. This second half is
   often overlooked but is vital: feeling *useful and needed* is a deep human need, and children who
   only ever receive (and never contribute) can feel strangely insignificant.

**Why it matters so much.** Feeling that you matter is foundational to wellbeing across life. For a
child, it underpins self-esteem (I'm worth something), security (I belong here), resilience (someone
cares whether I'm okay), and even behaviour (a child who feels they matter has less need to grab
significance through misbehaviour). It's closely tied to belonging and to the sense of "being seen." In
a busy, distracted world, many children quietly feel unseen — and helping a child feel they matter is a
direct investment in their whole mental health.

**How to help a child feel valued ("I am noticed and important"):**
- **Give focused, undivided attention** — even short bursts of truly present time (phone away, fully
  there) say "you matter to me" more than hours of distracted presence.
- **Listen — really listen** — take their thoughts, feelings, and opinions seriously; let them finish;
  show their inner world interests you.
- **Notice and remember the details** of their life — their friends, worries, passions. Being *known*
  is a powerful form of mattering.
- **Delight in them** — let your face light up for them; children read whether they're a joy or a
  burden. Aim for far more delight than correction.
- **Show love is unconditional** — that they matter to you regardless of achievements or behaviour.

**How to help a child feel they add value ("I am needed and make a difference"):**
- **Give real responsibility and jobs** — genuine contributions to the family (not busywork) that are
  noticed and appreciated: "thank you, that really helped us."
- **Let them help and care for others** — helping a sibling, a pet, a neighbour, the family; caring for
  others builds a sense of significance and empathy at once.
- **Show them their impact** — point out the difference they make ("you cheered your brother right up").
- **Ask their opinion and involve them** in family decisions where appropriate — being consulted says
  "your voice counts here."

**Watch for the child who feels unseen.** In families, it's easy for a quieter child, a middle child, or
a child during a busy or stressful period to slip into feeling they don't matter as much. Make sure each
child gets one-on-one time, is noticed for who they are (not compared or lost in the group), and hears —
in words and actions — that they are important and make a difference. A little deliberate attention here
prevents a lot of pain and misbehaviour.

**This is protective, not indulgent.** Helping a child feel they matter isn't spoiling them or inflating
their ego — it's meeting a fundamental need that protects their mental health and builds their character.
A child who genuinely feels valued *and* that they add value grows into someone secure, resilient,
empathetic, and purposeful.

**The one-line version.** Mattering — feeling both valued ("I'm noticed and important") and that you add
value ("I'm needed and make a difference") — is one of the strongest protectors of a child's wellbeing,
resilience, and behaviour, so build it with focused attention, real listening, delight, unconditional
love, genuine responsibility, and chances to help and contribute — making sure no child feels unseen.
