# Help Your Young Child Develop Self-Control

Help your young child begin to develop self-control and rely less on aggressive behaviors to communicate needs and feelings.

---

## Understanding Aggressive Behaviors in Babies

Although babies sometimes do things that appear and feel aggressive (like grabbing hair), they do not yet have the thinking skills to act hurtfully on purpose.  

- If your **6-month-old bites** you on the arm or your **12-month-old hits** you, it is not because they are trying to “get you.”  
- They are not yet able to control their feelings or use words to communicate their thoughts.  
- Over time, they learn right from wrong when you are clear and consistent with rules.

---

## Toddlers and Independence

Starting at about **18 months**, toddlers begin to realize they are separate from their parents and are eager to act independently.  

- They have **limited self-control** and have not yet learned to wait, share, or take turns.  
- While they are learning more words every day, they still rely heavily on actions to communicate.  

When toddlers are angry, frustrated, tired, or overwhelmed, they may:  
- Hit  
- Push  
- Slap  
- Grab  
- Kick  
- Bite  

These actions are their way of saying:  
- *I’m mad!*  
- *I’m exhausted!*  
- *I’m over my limit and need a break.*

## First, Consider Your Family

No two children or families are alike. Reflect on these questions to apply the strategies effectively:  

- What kinds of situations usually lead to your child acting aggressively? Why do you think this is?  
- When your child acts in ways that seem aggressive, how do you typically react?  
- Do you think this reaction is helpful to your child or not? Why?

## Respond in Age-Appropriate Ways

Help your young child begin to develop self-control and rely less on aggressive behaviors to communicate needs and feelings.

### From Birth to 12 Months
- Set limits in a clear, firm voice (without anger).  
- Redirect your baby’s attention.  
  - If she’s pulling on your hair, hold out a toy instead.  
  - If she is playing with the TV remote, offer her a toy with buttons to push.  

### From 12 Months and On
When toddlers are aggressive, it usually means they are out of control and need help to calm down before learning can take place.  

**Strategies to help your child:**
- **Stay calm.** The calmer you are, the more quickly your child will calm down.  
- **Recognize feelings or goals.** Example:  
  > *You want to stay longer at the playground, and are mad we have to leave. It’s okay to be mad, but it is not okay to hit Mommy. Hitting hurts.*  
- **Use gestures with words.** Pair a calm, firm voice with a “stop” or “no-no” gesture.  
- **Offer alternatives.** Example: Instead of throwing balls indoors, give a sponge ball or go outside for practice.  
- **Try a distraction.** Ignore the tantrum and redirect attention to something unexpected (bird outside, favorite book, or toy).  
- **Suggest healthy outlets for emotions.** Encourage jumping, kicking a ball, ripping paper, cuddling a teddy bear, etc.  
- **Help your child take a break.** Provide a safe, quiet space. This is not punishment—it helps children learn to soothe themselves.  
  - When your child calms down, praise them: *“Great job calming yourself down!”*
