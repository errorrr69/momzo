# Kids & Screen Time: How to Use the 5 C's of Media Guidance

Parenting around media can feel overwhelming. Technology changes quickly, and many parents didn't grow up with today's social media and video platforms. That makes it harder to know how to teach children to use digital media in the ways we would teach them to safely ride a bike or drive a car.

Unlike food or toys, technology isn’t subject to safety testing. We expect products our children use—like food or toys—to be tested for safety. But there aren't any safety regulations around technology designs right now. This means kids are using platforms and apps that might have been designed for adults—not children at their different stages of development.

---

## The 5 C's: Moving Beyond the 2-Hour Rule for Screen Time

We might wish for a simple solution or set of rules to follow, like the 2-hour screen time limit recommended in years past. But these don't address all of the things children and teens need to have a healthy relationship with media and maintain emotional well-being.

To help, the **American Academy of Pediatrics Center of Excellence on Social Media and Youth Mental Health** developed a new approach. The **5 C's media use guidance** offers easy-to-remember, age-based tips. It's based on research and supports family relationships, social-emotional development, and mental health.

---

## What Are the 5 C's and How Do They Work?

The 5 C's of media use are based on:
- **Child**
- **Content**
- **Calm**
- **Crowding Out**
- **Communication**

Guidance for each age group starts with information about developmental stages and how this influences media use. It also describes healthy media habits to strive for at home.

---

### 1. CHILD
Because children are all different, they don't each have the same risks and benefits from media. Parents should think:
- Who is your child, what is their personality, and how does this influence what media they are attracted to?  
- Do they seek out wild content, or does media support a special artistic or musical talent?  
- Is social media feeding their social anxiety, or do they have a supportive friend group online?  

---

### 2. CONTENT
Research shows that **content quality** shapes whether kids have positive or negative relationships with media.  
- Learn about what your kids play or watch.  
- Help them think about which videos and games have too much violence, rude role-modeling, unrealistic beauty standards, or commercialism.  
- Use **Common Sense Media** to find good replacement videos and games.  

---

### 3. CALM
All kids need strategies to manage strong emotions or fall asleep. Sometimes media becomes their main go-to.  
- Talk to friends, pediatricians, or therapists for other calming strategies.  
- Encourage non-digital ways to calm their brains and bodies.  

---

### 4. CROWDING OUT
Media can crowd out other important activities.  
- Focus not just on reducing screen time, but on **what you want to get back**: family time, sleep, outdoor play, or hobbies.  
- Recognize that digital media has "hooks" that keep us online longer than intended.  
- Create a plan for when and where media is used daily.  

---

### 5. COMMUNICATION
Talk about media early and often.  
- Builds digital literacy.  
- Helps identify when your child or teen is struggling.  
- Take a deep breath, be open-minded, and ask questions.  
- Reduces guilt and increases problem-solving mindsets.  

---

# Kids & Screen Time: 5 C's Questions for Toddlers & Preschoolers

From ages **2 to 4 years**, young children are learning a lot. They are more talkative, want to master new things, and can finally understand some educational content. This is also a time of big emotions and learning how to manage them. Media should not crowd out these developmental tasks.

---

## 5 C's Questions for Parents of Toddlers & Preschoolers

### 1. Child
- Who is your child, how do they react to media, and what are their motivations for using it?  
- Toddlers differ: some are impulsive and curious, others shy and slow to warm up.  
- Some demand media frequently, others let it go easily.  
- Knowing how your child reacts helps you set healthy boundaries.  

---

### 2. Content
- What is worth their attention?  
- Educational programs like **PBS KIDS, Sesame Street, Blue's Clues, Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood** can teach facts, healthy behaviors, and emotional management.  
- Limit YouTube cartoons or influencer content—often heavy on marketing or poor role modeling.  
- If using YouTube, make a playlist of high-quality videos and **turn off autoplay**.  

---

### 3. Calm
- How do they calm their emotions or go to sleep?  
- This age brings big tantrums—help children learn emotions and manage them:  
  - Stay calm yourself.  
  - Label emotions ("It's okay to feel sad/angry/frustrated").  
  - Help them calm down.  
- Avoid distracting them with devices whenever they’re upset or bored.  
- Keep screens for regular times or long trips.  
- Establish calming bedtime routines with reading and snuggling.  
- Role model calm activities.  

---

### 4. Crowding Out
- What does media get in the way of?  
- At this age, screens may crowd out:  
  - Playing  
  - Moving their body  
  - Exploring nature  
  - Looking at books  
  - Chatting with siblings and parents  
- These activities build social and brain skills for preschool and kindergarten success.  
- Set aside daily time to play, read, or go outside.  
- **Avoid open-access to tablets or phones.**  

---

### 5. Communication
- How can you talk about media to raise a smart and responsible child?  
- Toddlers and preschoolers understand much more language now.  
- Talk about what they see in videos—good vs. bad behavior in characters or ads.  
- If they’re watching something interesting, discuss it and do related activities together.
